Who Is a Good Fit for Implants?

Most adults with a missing tooth and healthy gums can be good candidates. You need enough bone to hold the post, and your gums need to be free of active disease. Age is not the issue people think it is; plenty of patients in their seventies and eighties do very well with implants. Smoking and diabetes that is not under control can slow healing, so the team talks with you about both. If bone is thin, a graft can often fix that. The consult is where all of this gets sorted out, with X-rays and an honest talk.

Dental Implants

An implant is a small titanium post that takes the place of a tooth root. It goes into the jawbone, and over a few months the bone grows around it and locks it in place. Then a crown, bridge, or denture is fixed on top. The new tooth chews like a real one, looks like a real one, and stays put. It also keeps the jawbone from shrinking, which is what happens when a tooth has been gone for a long time. That bone support helps your face keep its shape and your other teeth stay where they belong.

Single Missing Tooth Replacement

One missing tooth can shift the whole bite. The teeth on either side drift toward the gap, food gets stuck, and chewing feels uneven. A single implant and crown fills that one space and leaves the teeth next to it alone. That is the big plus over a bridge, which needs work on two healthy teeth. The crown is shaped and shaded to match your smile, so most people cannot tell which tooth was replaced.

Multiple Tooth Replacement

When a few teeth in a row are missing, implants can hold a bridge that never comes out. It does not slide, click, or need glue. You can bite, talk, and laugh without thinking about it. The team checks your bone and your bite, then builds the bridge to fit. A fixed bridge feels much closer to your own teeth than a removable partial does.

Full Mouth Implants

Some people have lost most or all of their teeth and want a fresh start. Full mouth implants can hold a full set of teeth that stay in place all day. The team talks through how many posts you need, what the new teeth will look like, and how the work is staged. It is a bigger job, but the change in daily life is big too: eating what you like, speaking clearly, and smiling without hiding your mouth.

All on 4 Treatments

All on 4 holds a full arch of teeth with four posts. The back posts tilt to find the strongest bone, which helps when bone is thin in spots. Many patients leave with temporary teeth the same day, so there is no stretch without a smile. If you wear dentures and are tired of them slipping, All on 4 is worth asking about at your consult.

All on 6 Treatments

All on 6 adds two more posts for extra support. That spreads the chewing load and can give you the firmest bite possible. It fits when your bone can hold the extra posts. The team looks at your X-rays and your bite, then tells you plainly which choice suits your mouth. Both choices give you teeth that stay put.

Bone Grafting

Bone thins after a tooth comes out. If there is not enough left to hold a post, a graft can build it back up. The team adds graft material where the bone is weak, and your own bone grows into the area over a few months. It is a routine part of implant care, done in the office, and it sets the implant up to last.

Dental Ridge Augmentation

After a tooth comes out, the ridge of bone that held it can sink and narrow. Ridge augmentation rebuilds that ridge so an implant has a solid home. The team does this work in the office, then lets the area heal before the implant goes in. It is one more way to make sure the new tooth sits right and looks natural.

Crown Lengthening

Now and then a tooth sits too low under the gum for a crown to grip it well. Crown lengthening gently shapes the gum and bone so more of the tooth shows. That gives the crown a firm base and a clean edge, and it evens out the gum line. It is a small step that makes a real difference in how the final tooth looks.

Dental Implant Crown Repair

Crowns on implants are strong, but they can chip, crack, or loosen. If yours feels odd or wobbles, call the office rather than waiting. The team can tighten it, fix it, or make a new one, and they will check the post under it too. Quick care now can keep a small problem from turning into a lost implant.

General Dentistry

Good implant care starts with a healthy mouth. General dentistry covers checkups, cleanings, X-rays, and gum care, and it keeps the teeth around your implant strong. The team checks your gums at every visit, because healthy gums are what hold an implant in place for years. Come in twice a year, and small issues get fixed before they grow.

Teeth Cleanings

Cleanings keep your gums healthy, and healthy gums keep implants healthy. The hygienist clears plaque and tartar, checks the gum line around your implant, and polishes your teeth. Tell the hygienist you have an implant so they use the right tools around it. Twice a year is the right pace for most people.

Dental Exams

At an exam, the team looks at your teeth, gums, bite, and jaw, and takes X-rays when they are due. For implant patients, the exam includes a check of the post and crown. Afterward, they go over what they found and what, if anything, needs care. You leave with a clear plan.

Invisalign®

Some implant patients also want straighter teeth. Invisalign clear aligners can line up the teeth around an implant so the bite feels even and the smile looks its best. The team can plan aligners and implants together, in the right order, so each step helps the next one. Ask about it at your consult if straightening is on your mind too.

Cosmetic Dentistry

An implant crown should look like it grew there. The team matches the shade, shape, and size to your other teeth, and checks how it looks when you smile and talk. Whitening, veneers, and bonding can also be part of the plan, so the whole smile looks even. The goal is a tooth that works well and looks like yours.

Dentures

Root Canal Therapy

Not every sore tooth needs to come out. A root canal can save a tooth when the tissue inside it is hurt or infected. The team cleans the inside of the tooth, seals it, and covers it with a crown. Most patients say it feels like a filling visit. Saving a tooth is often the best choice when it can be done well.

Wisdom Tooth Removal

Wisdom teeth can crowd a mouth and make it hard to keep teeth clean. The team takes X-rays to see how yours sit, then talks about taking them out if they are likely to cause pain or push other teeth out of line. Healing takes a few days of soft food and rest, and the team gives you clear notes to follow at home.

Tooth Extractions

When a tooth cannot be saved, taking it out gently is the kind thing to do. The team pulls the tooth with care for your comfort, then talks with you about replacing it, often with an implant. You will leave with steps for healing and a date to talk about the next step.

TMJ Treatments

Jaw pain, clicking, and headaches can come from the jaw joint, called the TMJ. The team checks your bite and jaw movement, then suggests care that may include a night guard, bite work, or simple home steps. Many people feel better once their bite is balanced and the jaw can rest.

Frequently Asked Questions

Will I be asleep during the implant procedure?

Most implant work is done with local numbing, so you are awake but the area feels nothing. If you feel nervous, talk with the team about your choices for staying calm. They will tell you what fits your case and your comfort.

How long until I can eat normally again?

For the first few days after the post is placed, stick to soft foods like soup, eggs, yogurt, and pasta. Once the area heals and the crown is on, you can eat most foods you like, just as you would with a natural tooth.

How do I clean around an implant?

Brush twice a day and clean between your teeth with floss or a small brush, just like your other teeth. The hygienist will show you the best tools for the spot around your implant. Twice-a-year cleanings keep the gum around it healthy.

Do implants come with any kind of guarantee?

The team stands behind its work and will talk with you about what is covered and for how long at your consult. What matters most is care at home: brushing, flossing, and twice-a-year visits are what keep an implant healthy for years.

Can I get a second opinion before I decide?

Yes, and the team welcomes it. Bring your X-rays and your questions to the consult, and they will give you a straight answer about what you need and what it will cost. A second opinion is a smart step before any big dental work.

How much do implants cost?
